Scroll through the day the way you'd live it. Keep an eye on the number in the corner. It's the only thing keeping count.

Start the day

7:12 AMGetting ready

Same as every morning.

Shower, coffee, keys, out the door. The sun is only just over the rooftops and the UV is sitting at 1. Nothing to think about yet.

8:40 AMTen minutes to the station

No hat, no sunscreen.

It's warm but not hot, so sunscreen doesn't cross your mind. Your shadow still stretches down the footpath. It doesn't feel like the sun is doing much yet.

Cancer Council says to protect your skin whenever the UV is 3 or above. UV has nothing to do with temperature, so a mild morning still counts.

12:30 PMLunch in the park

The bench in full sun was free.

The shady ones under the trees were taken, so you sat in the open for forty minutes. Nothing on your skin. You didn't feel a thing, and you never do while it's happening.

Your shadow, midday

2:15 PMDown at the beach

Still going.

A swim, a kick on the sand, a couple of hours on a towel. The sea breeze keeps you cool and the sky never looks dangerous. Sand and water bounce UV back at you, so you're copping it from above and below.

In a Victorian summer, UV can damage your skin in as little as 11 minutes.

6:45 PMHome

Nothing hurts.

No burn, no sting, nothing red in the mirror. If something had gone wrong today you'd know, wouldn't you? Not with UV. The damage builds up over time, and it can't be undone.

Today didn't leave a mark you could see. This is what years of days like it look like up close. UV damage adds up over a lifetime, and every unprotected hour counts towards it.

Tomorrow

None of today had to be invisible.

Nothing about today was extreme. It was a normal summer Tuesday, and the UV still added up. Protecting yourself is simple, and it works best when you do all five together.

Slipon clothing that covers as much skin as you can, like a shirt with long sleeves and a collar
Slopon SPF50+ sunscreen 20 minutes before you head out, and reapply at least every two hours
Slapon a broad brim, legionnaire or bucket hat that shades your face, neck and ears
Seekshade, but keep covering up. UV bounces off sand, water and concrete
Slideon close fitting wraparound sunglasses that meet the Australian standard

Tomorrow will be an ordinary day too. That's exactly why it's worth doing.
