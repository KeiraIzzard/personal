Start the day

7:12 AM — Getting ready

Same as every morning.

Shower, shirt, keys, out the door. The sun's barely up over the rooftops. There's nothing here to think about yet — and there isn't meant to be.

8:40 AM — Ten minutes to the station

No jacket, no hat.

It's warm, not hot — nothing that feels like it needs sunscreen. Your shadow's still long across the footpath. It doesn't look like much. It rarely does at this point.

A UV Index of 3 is already enough to start damaging unprotected skin in under half an hour.

12:30 PM — Lunch outside

The bench in full sun was free.

So that's where you sat. Forty minutes, no shade, nothing on your skin. You didn't feel a thing — you never do while it's actually happening.

Your shadow, midday

2:15 — 4:00 PM — Still outside

Still going.

Sport, a walk, mates in the backyard — hours out here and the sky never once looked dangerous. Your skin has no way of telling you the number's this high. It just keeps a tally either way.

6:45 PM — Home

Nothing hurts.

No burn, no sting, nothing red in the mirror. You'd know if something had gone wrong today — wouldn't you? Skin damage doesn't send a receipt. It just banks it, and shows up on its own schedule, years from now.

Tomorrow

None of today had to be invisible.

Nothing about today was extreme. That's the point — it was a completely normal Tuesday, and the exposure added up anyway. The fix isn't complicated either.

Slipon a shirt that covers your shoulders
Slopon SPF30+ before you leave, reapply after sweat or a swim
